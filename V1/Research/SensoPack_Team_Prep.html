Big picture (everyone must know)

Problem: India is the world's largest shrimp exporter, but shrimp spoils very fast and cold chains often break. Normal packaging only protects — it cannot tell if the food is still fresh.

Our idea (SensoPack): one smart, biodegradable film that both protects the shrimp and reports its freshness (a colour change you can see, plus a sensor). A reusable NFC tag + cloud AI then predict shelf life and give a simple verdict — Safe, Caution, or Unsafe.

Why new: rivals do only one thing (protect OR report), often costly or with plastic waste. SensoPack combines protection + sensing + biodegradable + low cost in one film.

Honest stage: today we have a working lab-scale bioplastic (biopolymer) film (TRL 3–4). Chitosan from shrimp-shell waste is our target material; the sensor, the electronic tag and the AI are designed — our next steps.

Key words — simple meanings

TermSimple meaning
TVB-NSmelly nitrogen gases (like ammonia) from protein breakdown. More = more spoiled. Limit ≈ 30–35 mg/100 g.
TMAThe "fishy smell" gas from bacteria. Limit ≈ 3–5 mg/100 g.
pH shiftSpoiling shrimp becomes less acidic (pH rises above 7.5). We can measure it.
Anthocyanin indicatorA natural colour that changes with pH. As gases raise the pH, it goes purple → green → brown. Freshness by eye.
MIP sensorMolecularly Imprinted Polymer — an artificial "lock" that fits one molecule (TMA). Like a synthetic antibody.
CMOS ASIC (130 nm)A tiny, cheap, very-low-power chip that reads the sensor and makes clean digital data.
NFCWireless chip read by tapping a phone. The read needs no battery — the phone powers it.
ChitosanNatural film material from shrimp/crab shell waste; biodegradable, food-safe. Our target material — current lab film is a bioplastic.
Bioplastic / biopolymerA biodegradable, plastic-like film from biological material. This is our current prototype.
MAPModified Atmosphere Packaging — changes the gas inside to slow spoilage. Protects but cannot report freshness.
TRL 3–4Technology Readiness Level. 3–4 = working lab prototype / proof of concept. We are here.

Jaspreet — Slides 5, 6

Your job: prove we understand the science of spoilage, and that our way to sense it is real and can be made at scale.

SLIDE 5 — Four-stage spoilage cascadeJaspreet
Say this"Thank you, Naveen. Now the science. Shrimp does not spoil in one step. It spoils in four stages. First, the muscle becomes stiff. Second, its own enzymes break it down. Third, bacteria grow and make gases like TMA and ammonia. Fourth, the fat turns bad and black spots come. Each stage makes some chemicals we can measure. These chemicals are our sensing targets. This science is from a 2023 research paper."

Understand this

  • The four stages: 1) Rigor mortis (muscle stiffens); 2) Autolysis (its own enzymes break tissue into amino acids); 3) Microbial spoilage (bacteria like Pseudomonas make TMA, ammonia, biogenic amines); 4) Lipid peroxidation & melanosis (fats go rancid, black spots).
  • Biomarkers & thresholds: TVB-N 30–35, TMA 3–5 (mg/100 g), pH above 7.5. These rise as it spoils, so measuring them = measuring freshness.
  • Reference: Das & Mishra, 2023 — keep this name ready.

Likely questions

Q: What are the four stages?

A: Rigor mortis, autolysis, microbial spoilage, and lipid peroxidation & melanosis.

Q: What is a biomarker, and why measure it?

A: A chemical released as shrimp spoils (TMA, TVB-N, pH). They have known spoilage limits, so measuring them gives an objective freshness reading.

Q: What are the threshold numbers?

A: TVB-N 30–35 and TMA 3–5 mg per 100 g, pH above 7.5.

Q: Where is this science from?

A: Published research — Das & Mishra, 2023.

SLIDE 6 — Detect · Sense · ManufactureJaspreet
Say this"How do we detect these chemicals? We track three markers — TMA, TVB-N, and pH. For sensing, our design uses a special polymer sensor, a low-power chip, and NFC. NFC means you can read it with just a phone — no battery needed. The film is very thin, under 20 microns. It can be made on normal packaging machines. No new factory is needed. Now Viraj will continue."

Understand this

  • Detection: we target TMA, TVB-N and pH — all validated freshness markers that rise together.
  • Sensing design: a MIP sensor (artificial lock that binds TMA selectively), a 130 nm CMOS chip (cleans the signal, ultra-low power), and NFC (phone-tap read, no battery for the read).
  • Manufacturing: film under 20 microns, made by roll-to-roll / slot-die, works with existing heat-sealers — no new factory.
  • Say "our sensing design uses…" — these parts are designed; the built item is the film (Viraj, Slide 9).

Likely questions

Q: What is a MIP sensor, simply?

A: A Molecularly Imprinted Polymer — an artificial lock shaped to fit one molecule like TMA. It binds it selectively, like a synthetic antibody.

Q: How can NFC work with no battery?

A: For the read, the phone's own signal powers the NFC tag. No battery is needed for the freshness read.

Q: Can this really be mass-produced?

A: By design, yes — a thin film made by roll-to-roll coating that fits existing heat-sealers, so no new factory. Holding quality on industrial rolls is a known scale-up step.

Q: Why these three markers?

A: TMA, TVB-N and pH are standard, validated seafood-freshness indicators that rise together as it spoils, giving a reliable picture.

Golden rules for answering judges
  • Answer in one clear sentence first, then one line of support. Do not ramble.
  • Only use facts on the slides or in this handbook. If it is not here, do not invent it.
  • Separate "built" from "designed". Film (bioplastic) = built. Chitosan, sensor, tag, AI = designed / next step.
  • "Not yet" is a strong answer: "We have not tested that yet — it is our next step."
  • Any of the four can answer for the whole project. If one is unsure, another steps in calmly.
  • Speak slowly and simply. Short sentences beat fancy words.
Never say these (not true yet / not on slides)
  • Do not say the sensor, NFC tag, chip or AI are built or tested. Only the film is built.
  • Do not call the current prototype "chitosan" — the built film is a bioplastic (biopolymer); chitosan is our design target.
  • Do not give any accuracy %, shelf-life-extension number, or pilot result — we have none yet.
  • Do not say "patented", "certified", "approved", or "guaranteed".
  • Do not invent customers, orders, funding, or awards. Do not exaggerate ("revolution", "millions of lives").
